1. Water: where the flood was, and what a model expects

Observed flood extent (Copernicus EMS Rapid Mapping): flood outlines drawn by analysts from satellite images. It is the only observation of the real 2021 flood in the challenge data. Each map is a snapshot at the satellite pass, not the peak, and only areas requested by authorities (AOIs) were mapped: outside them, “not flooded” means “not mapped”. Here the outline merges every “observed event” file: the delineation (DEL) products and also grading (GRA) products and later monitoring maps, so it is the maximum extent seen across several dates, not one snapshot. Only the flood-extent polygons are used: no depth and no damage grade.

Modelled water depth (EFAS river flood hazard): model-based depth for a 10-, 100- and 500-year flood across Europe. It is a model of generic floods, not the 2021 event. Small rivers (catchment under 500 km²) are not modelled, and the model was run on 1990–2016 data.

2. Population: who lived there

GHS-POP R2023A, epoch 2020 (JRC): estimated residents per grid cell, 30 arc-seconds (about 1 km), modelled from census data and built-up area. 2020 is used for the 2021 event (the 2025 raster is a projection). Method used here: each cell is weighted by the share of its area covered by the observed flood (10×10 sub-cells per cell). “Touched cells” counts whole cells the flood overlaps and is an upper bound.

3. Buildings: what was there

GHS-OBAT R2024A (JRC): one record per building, as a point (footprint centroid), reference year 2020, derived from Overture Maps building footprints. Method used here: all building points inside the observed flood extent or within 15 m of it (the margin counts buildings at the flood edge, whose point may fall just outside the polygon); read tile by tile from the full country files. Buildings are counted, not measured: footprint area is deliberately not used. Use code: 1 = residential, 2 = non-residential, 0 = outside the built-up domain (per the OBAT documentation); all are counted. Construction epoch (1–5): before 1980, 1980–90, 1990–2000, 2000–10, 2010–20.

4. Communes and response tiers

Aim: help a regional civil protection authority decide where to concentrate preparedness and alert messages. The unit is the commune (Eurostat GISCO LAU 2023, an external source, which also gives place names and official population). Exposure is the number of GHS-POP residents in the observed flood, weighted by the flooded share of each cell; the rate divides it by the commune's GHS-POP total.

Tier rule (our draft; thresholds are relative to this partial sample, not official limits). High: at least 83 residents exposed per 1,000 inhabitants or at least 328 residents exposed (top 10% of the 289 flooded communes). Medium: at least 13 per 1,000 or 29 residents (top 40%). High is 1B when at least 50% of the observed flood lies inside the EFAS 100-year zone, otherwise 1A. No data when a low-exposure commune has under 50% of its area inside a mapped area: unknown, not safe.

The four core layers are European public data (JRC, Copernicus), free to use with credit, read from the challenge bucket gs://datasets_challenge/working_data. The observed flood merges all EMS “observed event” files, including grading (GRA) products and later monitoring maps, as one outline. Not covered: the non-flood EMS layers (roads, built-up areas, hydrography), damage assessment, and any dataset beyond those listed. Interpretation choices (what counts as flood, buffer, thresholds) are recorded in config.json. Check each figure against its source before using it in a policy brief.
